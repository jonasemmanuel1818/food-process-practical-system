1. Introduction

Filtration is a physical separation operation used to separate insoluble solid particles from a liquid by passing the mixture through a porous filtering medium.

During gravity filtration, the liquid passes through the pores of the filter paper while the insoluble solid particles are retained on the filter paper. The liquid that passes through the filter is called the filtrate, while the material retained by the filter paper is called the residue.

Food Processing Application

Filtration is used in food processing for clarification of beverages, removal of suspended particles, separation of solids from liquid food products and treatment of process streams.

3. Materials and Equipment

  • Sample mixture containing liquid and insoluble particles.
  • Filtration funnel.
  • Filter paper.
  • Receiving beaker.
  • Laboratory stand or support.
  • Digital balance for mass measurement.
  • Stopwatch or timer.

4. Principle of Filtration

Filtration operates because the filter medium contains pores that allow the liquid phase to pass through while particles larger than the effective pore openings are retained.

The separation performance can be evaluated by comparing the initial mass of the sample with the mass of solids retained after filtration.

5. Filtrate and Residue

Filtrate
The liquid that passes through the filter medium during filtration.
Residue
The solid material retained on the filter paper after filtration.

The difference between filtrate and residue is important when interpreting the results of a filtration process.

6. Procedure

Step 1
Prepare the sample mixture containing liquid and insoluble solid particles.
Step 2
Place the filtration funnel in position.
Step 3
Install the filter paper inside the funnel.
Step 4
Place the receiving beaker directly below the funnel stem.
Step 5
Transfer the sample mixture into the filtration funnel.
Step 6
Allow gravity to drive the liquid through the filter paper.
Step 7
Observe the residue remaining on the filter paper and the filtrate collected below.
Step 8
Record the required measurements and calculate the results.

7. Safety Precautions

  • Handle laboratory glassware carefully.
  • Ensure that the filtration apparatus is stable before starting the experiment.
  • Avoid overfilling the filtration funnel.
  • Keep the working area clean and dry.
  • Follow laboratory safety instructions throughout the practical.

9. Filtration Calculations

Filtrate Mass

Filtrate Mass = Initial Sample Mass − Retained Solid Mass

Percentage of Solids Retained

Retention (%) = (Retained Solid Mass ÷ Initial Sample Mass) × 100

Separation Percentage

Separation (%) = (Filtrate Mass ÷ Initial Sample Mass) × 100
Important

The retained solid mass should not be greater than the initial sample mass.

10. Measurements and Results

The filtration simulation uses three main input measurements.

Measurement Description Unit
Initial Sample Mass Total mass of the sample before filtration. g
Retained Solid Mass Mass of solid material retained by the filter. g
Filtration Time Time required for the filtration process. seconds

11. Calculation Example

Consider a sample with an initial mass of 100 g and a retained solid mass of 25 g.

Filtrate Mass

Filtrate Mass = 100 − 25 = 75 g

Retention Percentage

Retention = (25 ÷ 100) × 100 = 25%

Separation Percentage

Separation = (75 ÷ 100) × 100 = 75%

These values demonstrate how the mass balance of the filtration process can be interpreted.

Key Learning Points

  • Filtration separates insoluble solid particles from a liquid using a porous filtering medium.
  • The liquid passing through the filter is called the filtrate.
  • The material retained by the filter paper is called the residue.
  • Gravity can drive liquid through the filter paper during gravity filtration.
  • Filtrate mass can be determined from the initial sample mass and retained solid mass.
  • Retention percentage describes the proportion of the initial sample retained as solid.
  • Filtration has applications in food processing, including clarification and removal of suspended particles.
