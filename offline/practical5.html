1. Introduction

Filtration is a physical separation process used to separate solid particles from a liquid by passing the mixture through a porous medium called a filter.

During filtration, the liquid that passes through the filter is called the filtrate, while the solid particles retained by the filter are called the residue or retained solid.

Filtration is widely used in food processing industries for clarification, removal of suspended particles and separation of solid materials from liquids.

Practical Focus

In this practical you will study gravity filtration and use a virtual filtration laboratory to understand the separation of filtrate and retained solids.

3. Materials and Equipment

The following equipment and materials are commonly used for gravity filtration:

  • Filter paper
  • Filter funnel
  • Beaker
  • Receiving container
  • Glass rod
  • Sample containing suspended solids
  • Balance for measuring mass

4. Principle of Filtration

Filtration operates on the difference in particle size between the solid particles and the liquid.

The filter paper contains small pores. Liquid can pass through these pores while particles larger than the pores are retained.

Liquid + Suspended Solids → Filtration → Filtrate + Retained Solids

Filtrate

The filtrate is the liquid that passes through the filter medium.

Retained Residue

The retained residue is the solid material that remains on the filter paper.

5. Filtrate and Retained Residue

Term Meaning
Filtrate Liquid that passes through the filter medium.
Retained Residue Solid particles retained on the filter medium.
Filter Medium Porous material used to separate the solid from the liquid.

6. Filtration Procedure

  1. Prepare the filtration equipment.
  2. Fold the filter paper appropriately.
  3. Place the filter paper inside the filtration funnel.
  4. Position the funnel above the receiving container.
  5. Measure the initial mass of the sample.
  6. Carefully transfer the sample into the filter.
  7. Allow the liquid to pass through the filter paper.
  8. Observe the filtrate collected in the receiving container.
  9. Observe the solid material retained by the filter paper.
  10. Record the required measurements.
  11. Calculate the filtration results.

7. Safety Precautions

  • Handle glass equipment carefully.
  • Keep the working area clean.
  • Do not force liquid through the filter.
  • Avoid spilling the sample.
  • Use appropriate laboratory protective equipment.
  • Dispose of waste materials according to laboratory procedures.

9. Filtration Calculations

Filtrate Mass

Filtrate Mass = Initial Sample Mass − Retained Solid Mass

Percentage of Solids Retained

Retention Percentage = (Retained Solid Mass ÷ Initial Sample Mass) × 100

Separation Percentage

Separation Percentage = (Filtrate Mass ÷ Initial Sample Mass) × 100

11. Calculation Example

Assume that the initial sample mass is 100 g and the retained solid mass is 25 g.

Filtrate Mass

100 − 25 = 75 g

Retention Percentage

(25 ÷ 100) × 100 = 25%

Separation Percentage

(75 ÷ 100) × 100 = 75%

Key Learning Points

  • Filtration separates insoluble solid particles from a liquid using a porous filtering medium.
  • The liquid passing through the filter is called the filtrate.
  • The material retained by the filter paper is called the residue.
  • Gravity can drive liquid through the filter paper during gravity filtration.
  • Filtrate mass can be determined from the initial sample mass and retained solid mass.
  • Retention percentage describes the proportion of the initial sample retained as solid.
  • Filtration has applications in food processing, including clarification and removal of suspended particles.
