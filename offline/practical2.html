Centrifugation and Sieve Analysis

1. Introduction

Physical separation is an important operation in food processing. It involves separating components of a mixture based on differences in their physical properties.

In this practical, physical separation is studied using two methods: centrifugation and sieve analysis.

Centrifugation uses centrifugal force to separate components according to differences in density, while sieve analysis separates particles according to particle size.

2. Objectives

  1. To understand the principle of physical separation in food processing.
  2. To understand the principle and application of centrifugation.
  3. To calculate centrifugal force and understand the relationship between RPM and RCF.
  4. To understand sieve analysis as a particle size separation method.
  5. To determine the distribution of particles according to sieve size.

4. Centrifugation

Centrifugation is a physical separation process in which centrifugal force is used to separate components of a mixture.

When a sample is rotated at high speed, particles experience centrifugal force. Components with greater density tend to move outward and sediment more rapidly than components with lower density.

Principle of Centrifugation

The separation effect in a centrifuge depends on factors such as rotational speed, radius of rotation, particle properties and the difference in density between components.

Important:

The centrifuge should be properly balanced before operation. Uneven loading can cause vibration and may damage the equipment.

5. Relative Centrifugal Force (RCF)

Relative centrifugal force describes the centrifugal force applied to a sample relative to the gravitational force.

RCF = 1.118 × 10-5 × r × RPM2

Where:

  • RCF = Relative Centrifugal Force
  • r = radius of rotation in centimetres
  • RPM = revolutions per minute

6. Centrifugation Procedure

Step 1: Prepare the sample and transfer it into centrifuge tubes.
Step 2: Balance the centrifuge tubes by ensuring that opposite tubes have approximately equal masses.
Step 3: Place the balanced tubes into the centrifuge.
Step 4: Close the centrifuge securely.
Step 5: Set the required rotational speed and operating time.
Step 6: Start the centrifuge and allow the separation process to take place.
Step 7: Wait until the centrifuge has completely stopped before opening it.
Step 8: Remove the samples carefully and observe the separated components.

7. Sieve Analysis

Sieve analysis is a physical separation method used to determine particle-size distribution.

A sample is passed through a series of sieves having different mesh sizes. Larger particles are retained on sieves with larger openings while smaller particles pass through to lower sieves.

Principle:

Sieve analysis separates particles mainly according to particle size.

8. Sieve Analysis Procedure

Step 1: Prepare and weigh the sample.
Step 2: Arrange the sieves from the largest opening at the top to the smallest opening at the bottom.
Step 3: Place a collection container below the sieve set.
Step 4: Transfer the sample onto the upper sieve.
Step 5: Shake the sieve assembly for the required period.
Step 6: Remove each sieve carefully.
Step 7: Weigh the material retained on each sieve.
Step 8: Record the measurements and calculate the particle distribution.

9. Sieve Analysis Calculations

The mass retained on each sieve can be used to calculate the percentage retained.

% Retained = (Mass Retained / Total Sample Mass) × 100

The cumulative percentage retained is calculated by adding the percentage retained on the current sieve to all previous percentages.

% Passing = 100 − Cumulative % Retained

14. Safety Precautions

  • Wear appropriate laboratory protective equipment.
  • Ensure centrifuge tubes are properly balanced.
  • Do not open the centrifuge while it is rotating.
  • Inspect equipment before use.
  • Handle samples carefully.
  • Ensure the sieve assembly is properly secured before operation.
  • Keep the working area clean.
  • Report damaged equipment or unsafe conditions.

15. Key Learning Points

  • Physical separation uses differences in physical properties to separate components.
  • Centrifugation uses centrifugal force for separation.
  • RCF depends on the radius of rotation and rotational speed.
  • Sieve analysis separates particles according to particle size.
  • The mass retained on different sieves can be used to determine particle-size distribution.
