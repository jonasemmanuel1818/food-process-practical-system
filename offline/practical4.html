1. Introduction

Drying is an important food processing operation used to remove moisture from food materials. Removal of moisture can improve storage stability and reduce the amount of water available for undesirable reactions and microbial growth.

This practical covers two important drying processes: spray drying and freeze drying.

Spray drying converts a liquid or slurry into a dry powder by atomizing the feed into a stream of hot air. Freeze drying removes water from a frozen product mainly through sublimation.

3. Spray Drying

Spray drying is a drying process in which a liquid feed is converted into a dry powder. The feed is atomized into small droplets and brought into contact with hot drying air.

Because the droplets have a large surface area, moisture can evaporate rapidly as the droplets move through the drying chamber.

Main Components of a Spray Dryer

  • Feed system
  • Atomizer
  • Drying chamber
  • Hot-air system
  • Cyclone separator
  • Powder collection system

4. Spray Drying Procedure

Step 1
Prepare the material to be spray dried.
Step 2
Check the spray dryer and make sure the equipment is ready for operation.
Step 3
Set the required inlet temperature.
Step 4
Introduce the feed into the spray dryer.
Step 5
Allow the feed to be atomized into droplets.
Step 6
Allow hot air to remove moisture from the droplets.
Step 7
Collect the resulting powder.
Step 8
Record the required temperatures, quantities and observations.

6. Dry-Matter Content

Dry-matter content represents the portion of a material that remains after moisture has been removed.

Dry-Matter Percentage

Dry Matter (%) = (Mass of Dry Matter ÷ Mass of Sample) × 100

The dry-matter content of the milk and milk powder can be determined and compared.

7. Calculation of Powder Collected

The amount of powder collected after spray drying should be recorded and compared with the amount of material processed.

Powder Collected = Final Mass of Collected Powder

Where appropriate, the collected powder can be used to determine the efficiency or recovery of the drying process.

8. Freeze Drying

Freeze drying is a drying process in which water is removed from a frozen product under reduced pressure.

The frozen water changes directly from a solid state to vapour without passing through the liquid state. This process is called sublimation.

Principle of Sublimation

Sublimation is the direct change of a substance from the solid state to the vapour state. In freeze drying, frozen water is removed from the product through this process.

9. Freeze-Drying Procedure

Step 1
Prepare the food sample for freeze drying.
Step 2
Freeze the product before the drying stage.
Step 3
Place the frozen product inside the drying chamber.
Step 4
Establish the required chamber pressure.
Step 5
Apply the required heating conditions.
Step 6
Allow sublimation to remove water from the frozen product.
Step 7
Record temperature, pressure and drying observations.

10. Effect of Product Thickness on Freeze-Drying Time

Product thickness affects the distance that moisture must travel during freeze drying. A thicker product generally requires a longer period for moisture to be removed compared with a thinner product under comparable conditions.

Practical Point

Product thickness should therefore be considered when comparing freeze-drying times.

Comparing the two helps identify differences between expected and observed drying behaviour.
