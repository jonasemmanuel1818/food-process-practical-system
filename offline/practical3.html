Welcome

This practical introduces you to thermal processing, heat penetration, determination of the cold point and interpretation of temperature-time data.

Important

Use your actual laboratory readings when performing calculations and recording results.

1. Introduction

Thermal processing is a food preservation method in which food is heated to a specified temperature for a specified period of time to reduce microorganisms and improve product safety and shelf stability.

During thermal processing, heat must penetrate from the heating medium into the food product. The point within the product that heats most slowly is known as the cold point.

Determining the cold point is important because it helps evaluate whether the entire product receives adequate thermal treatment.

2. Objectives

  1. Explain the concept of thermal processing.
  2. Understand the concept of the cold point.
  3. Determine the cold point of a processed product.
  4. Determine the heating curve of the product.
  5. Interpret temperature-time data.
  6. Perform basic thermal processing calculations.

3. Materials and Methods

Materials

  • Retort
  • Food sample containers
  • Thermocouples or temperature sensors
  • Data logger
  • Stopwatch
  • Retort baskets or holders
  • Measuring instruments
  • Personal protective equipment

Methods

  1. Place the food samples inside the retort.
  2. Position the temperature sensors at the required locations inside the product.
  3. Operate the retort according to the laboratory procedure.
  4. Record product temperature at regular time intervals.
  5. Compare temperature readings from different locations.
  6. Identify the location that heats most slowly as the cold point.

4. Precautions

  • Wear appropriate personal protective equipment.
  • Follow all laboratory instructions.
  • Do not operate the retort without proper instruction.
  • Handle hot equipment carefully.
  • Ensure temperature sensors are correctly positioned.
  • Record temperature readings accurately.
  • Do not touch hot surfaces directly.
  • Report damaged or malfunctioning equipment to the instructor.

5. Finding the Cold Point of the Retort

Procedure

  1. Place temperature sensors at different positions within the food sample.
  2. Start the thermal processing operation.
  3. Record the temperature of each sensor at regular intervals.
  4. Compare the temperature-time profiles.
  5. Identify the position with the lowest temperature or slowest heating response.
  6. Record this position as the cold point.
Expected Result

The cold point should be identified as the location within the product that heats most slowly during thermal processing.

6. Determining the Heating Curve

Procedure

  1. Record the initial product temperature.
  2. Record the retort temperature.
  3. Record product temperature at regular time intervals.
  4. Plot product temperature against processing time.
  5. Examine the resulting heating curve.

Heating Rate

Heating Rate = (Final Temperature − Initial Temperature) ÷ Processing Time

Temperature Difference

Key Learning Points

  • Thermal processing involves heating food for a specified temperature and time.
  • Heat must penetrate the food product during thermal processing.
  • The cold point is the location that heats most slowly.
  • Temperature-time data can be used to study heating behaviour.
  • Heating curves help illustrate changes in product temperature during processing.
  • Accurate temperature measurements are important for thermal processing analysis.
